# Funding Strategy

## Garden Route Integrated AgriHub — Nacirfa Dream Group

---

## 1. Overview

The AgriHub's capital requirement is structured in three sequential phases, each unlocking the next stage of scale rather than requiring the full amount upfront. This phased approach reduces risk for funders and allows performance in Phase 1 to de-risk the case for Phase 2 and Phase 3 capital.

| Phase | Focus | Capital Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Foundation & Proof of Concept | R4.85M |
| Phase 2 | Commercial Scale-Up | R4.50M |
| Phase 3 | Regional Hub Maturity | R3.15M |
| **Total** | | **R12.50M** |

---

## 2. Phase 1 — Foundation & Proof of Concept (R4.85M)

Establishes the core infrastructure: protected cultivation tunnels, water storage and purification, 5kW solar pumping, logistics groundwork, cold storage, and working capital to reach first commercial harvest.

**Target outcomes:** 15 tonnes of vegetables, 100,000 litres of water capacity, 5 direct jobs, 30 households served in Year 1.

**Suited funding sources:** founder/seed capital, angel or early-stage impact investors, agricultural development grants, and early-stage DFI facilities willing to fund proof-of-concept infrastructure.

## 3. Phase 2 — Commercial Scale-Up (R4.50M)

Expands protected cultivation, adds a packhouse and cold storage, expands the delivery fleet, and adds battery storage to firm up solar reliability.

**Target outcomes:** production scaling toward 60+ tonnes/year, first institutional supply agreements (schools, restaurants, retail), 15–20 direct jobs.

**Suited funding sources:** development finance institution (DFI) debt, commercial agricultural finance, blended finance structures combining concessional and commercial capital, reinvested Phase 1 cash flow.

## 4. Phase 3 — Regional Hub Maturity (R3.15M)

Adds a hydroponic production line, expands solar capacity to 75kW total, scales water logistics for community distribution, and expands the seedling nursery.

**Target outcomes:** 100+ tonnes/year, 5,000,000 litres of water distributed annually, 20–30 direct jobs, 300+ subscribing households — the AgriHub's full five-year target profile.

**Suited funding sources:** growth capital, follow-on DFI facilities, and retained earnings from Phase 2 operations. At this stage the model is intended to be a template ready for evaluation and replication in additional Eastern Cape regions.

---

## 5. Funding Instrument Mix

The AgriHub is designed to be funded through a blend of instruments rather than a single source, reflecting the different risk profiles of infrastructure capex versus working capital versus growth capital:

- **Grant / concessional capital** — best suited to Phase 1 proof-of-concept infrastructure where commercial lenders are typically reluctant to fund pre-revenue risk.
- **DFI and development bank debt** — suited to Phase 2 and Phase 3 infrastructure with a demonstrated revenue base to service debt.
- **Commercial agricultural finance** — equipment and vehicle finance where asset-backed lending is available.
- **Impact and strategic equity investors** — for investors seeking both financial return and measurable food security, water access, and employment impact.

---

## 6. Use of Funds Discipline

Each phase's capital is tied to a specific, auditable set of infrastructure line items (see the Financial Model's Capex & Funding schedule) rather than a general operating fund. This is intended to give funders line-of-sight from capital deployed to specific infrastructure delivered and specific KPI targets achieved.
